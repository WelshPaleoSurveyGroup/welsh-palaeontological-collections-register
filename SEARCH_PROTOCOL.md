# Search protocol

The aim is to build a reproducible, auditable register rather than an informal list of fossils.

## 1. Museum catalogues first

Prioritise direct institutional records because they provide catalogue numbers and repository-backed metadata.

Current priority sources include:

- Amgueddfa Cymru – Museum Wales Collections Online and palaeontology type database
- NHMUK Data Portal
- GB3D Type Fossils Online
- Sedgwick Museum collections
- British Geological Survey / National Geological Repository
- Oxford University Museum of Natural History
- Cambridge collections
- regional Welsh and border museums

Search by:

- locality;
- county/historic county;
- taxon;
- formation/member;
- collector;
- type status;
- catalogue-number clusters.

## 2. Google Scholar

Useful query patterns include:

```text
"Penarth" fossil specimen
"Lavernock" dinosaur museum
"Pant-y-ffynnon" NHMUK
"Wales" fossil holotype
"Glamorgan" trilobite museum
"Welsh" fossil collection
```

When a useful paper is found:

1. record it in the publication-candidate table;
2. inspect the specimen/material section;
3. extract catalogue numbers exactly;
4. follow references backward;
5. use "Cited by" to find later revisions;
6. verify specimen numbers against museum catalogues where possible.

Do not stop after the first page of results.

## 3. Web of Science and Scopus

Use these primarily for citation chaining and indexed recall. Search exact titles and important taxon/locality combinations.

If access is unavailable, mark the search as not completed rather than assuming there are no results.

## 4. Crossref

Use Crossref for bibliographic quality control: DOI, canonical title, author list, journal, year, and pagination. Do not treat bibliographic metadata as specimen evidence.

## 5. Biodiversity Heritage Library

Use BHL for historic descriptions, old taxonomic names, collectors, geological memoirs, and museum catalogues.

Search:

- historic county names;
- obsolete taxonomic names;
- collectors;
- locality variants;
- formation names;
- catalogue numbers when known.

OCR is imperfect. A zero-result search is not proof of absence.

## 6. Internet Archive and digitised monographs

Use these for historical volumes, proceedings, survey memoirs, museum catalogues, and old monographs. Record stable item/page URLs and page numbers.

## 7. Geology verification

Use source labels, primary literature, BGS resources, and mapped geology cautiously. Do not assign a highly specific formation solely because a broad historical locality plots on that unit today.

## 8. Promotion rule

A candidate becomes a permanent specimen record only when it is independently relocatable and sufficiently evidenced.

Preferred minimum:

```text
repository + catalogue number + Welsh provenance + source
```

## 9. Negative searches

Record searches that produce no useful result. This is important for reproducibility and prevents repeated work.
